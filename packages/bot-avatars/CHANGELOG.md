# Changelog

## 0.1.0

First release: eighteen types (clover, flower, triangle, square, blob, ghost, circle, drop, star, droid, mech, alien, hexagon, cat, cloud, pill, pebble, puddle), two faces (eyes, mouth), three states (default, working, sleeping) with head turns, hops, flips and cross-animated switches, palette colours with `color`, `brightness`, `saturation` and `ink` overrides, `size`, `speed`, `paused`, `seed`, `shading` (per-pixel plastic by default, crisp, smooth or flat) with `shadow`, `highlight`, `light`, `depth`, `rim` and `spread`. Eyes and head follow the pointer, a click hops and flips (`interactive`). Rendered on a 2D canvas as a lit extrusion; the rig and renderer are exported.

Performance pass before release: the plastic material rebuilds its lighting only once the light or view has moved a whole bin (numeric keys, no strings per frame), keeps its texels in canvases of its own (two in turn, which also fixes Safari showing another avatar's texture: WebKit reads a `drawImage` source when the frame flushes), picks the texture tier from the real device size (the old read of the context transform included the squash, so tiers flipped mid-hop and small avatars got the coarse tier), bakes forms one per idle slot (Safari has no `requestIdleCallback`; before, every type on a page baked in one go), and every mode sets slice transforms relative to the previous slice instead of save/transform/translate/restore. Crisp and smooth precompute their slice colours per palette; the theme is resolved per render, not per frame. `DrawConfig.dpr` tells the renderer the context's scale so it never reads the transform back. Eyes are stroked as short polylines (Safari drew the open eye's curve as a teardrop). The whirl is off by default; `whirl={1}` turns it on. On WebKit (Safari, every iOS browser) the plastic side slices are blitted from three sprites of the outline, redrawn only when the lighting rebuilds, because a conic-gradient fill there costs thirty times a flat one: a page of eighteen avatars went from about 19 ms to 1.6 ms of frame time in Safari, and the only difference from the vector path is a pixel of anti-aliasing along the side stack's silhouette. Chromium and Firefox keep filling the slices as vectors; `DrawConfig.sides` forces either path.
