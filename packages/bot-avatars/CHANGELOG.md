# Changelog

## 0.1.0

First release: eighteen types (clover, flower, triangle, square, blob, ghost, circle, drop, star, droid, mech, alien, hexagon, cat, cloud, pill, pebble, puddle), two faces (eyes, mouth), three states (default, working, sleeping) with head turns, hops, flips and cross-animated switches, palette colours with `color`, `brightness`, `saturation` and `ink` overrides, `size`, `speed`, `paused`, `seed`, `shading` (per-pixel plastic by default, crisp, smooth or flat) with `shadow`, `highlight`, `light`, `depth`, `rim` and `spread`. Eyes and head follow the pointer, a click hops and flips (`interactive`). The face is clipped to the front of the body, so a wide turn can never carry a feature off the silhouette. Settling back to idle from working takes a second and a fifth,, and a hop under way flies its whole arc, finishes its turn and lands with a jump's proper landing — the hold on the ground and the rise back — before the hops stop; every state switch eases on a sine. Idle, the head looks to a corner, stays a few seconds and then swings across to the opposite one — fifty degrees of turn either side, fourteen of rise and a slight tilt with it — the eyes running ahead of the turn (`turn` scales how far it goes to the side), and they draw taller looking up and shorter looking down. Rendered on a 2D canvas as a lit extrusion; the rig and renderer are exported.

Performance pass before release: the plastic material rebuilds its lighting only once the light or view has moved a whole bin (numeric keys, no strings per frame), keeps its texels in canvases of its own (two in turn, which also fixes Safari showing another avatar's texture: WebKit reads a `drawImage` source when the frame flushes), picks the texture tier from the real device size (the old read of the context transform included the squash, so tiers flipped mid-hop and small avatars got the coarse tier), bakes forms one per idle slot (Safari has no `requestIdleCallback`; before, every type on a page baked in one go), and every mode sets slice transforms relative to the previous slice instead of save/transform/translate/restore. Crisp and smooth precompute their slice colours per palette; the theme is resolved per render, not per frame. `DrawConfig.dpr` tells the renderer the context's scale so it never reads the transform back. Eyes are stroked as short polylines (Safari drew the open eye's curve as a teardrop). The whirl is off by default; `whirl={1}` turns it on. On WebKit (Safari, every iOS browser) the plastic side slices are blitted from three sprites of the outline, redrawn only when the lighting rebuilds, because a conic-gradient fill there costs thirty times a flat one: a page of eighteen avatars went from about 19 ms to 1.6 ms of frame time in Safari, and the only difference from the vector path is a pixel of anti-aliasing along the side stack's silhouette. Chromium and Firefox keep filling the slices as vectors; `DrawConfig.sides` forces either path. The idle flip (and a click's) is the working state's spinning hop on its own: the same sine arc, timing, stretch at the top, lean and shut eyes, with a crouch before take-off; its landing squash starts at the moment of contact and recovers without a wobble. Leaving the working state, the hop under way finishes whole and its landing fades, instead of hops shrinking with the state weight and a spin ending short of a turn. The lighting cross-fades between rebuilds, so a slow turn no longer steps a bin at a time. The canvas overscans 1.5× with the body a tenth of the box below its middle, so a spin hop is not cropped at the top. The mouth is one shape whose numbers blend across states, a morph instead of a cross-fade of three. `saturation` defaults to 1.5. The jump's numbers are props — `jumpHeight`, `jumpTime`, `jumpStretch`, `jumpSpin`, `jumpLean`, `jumpEvery`, `jumpLand` (when the landing squash begins, round the moment of contact), `jumpSquash`, `jumpSquashTime` and `jumpSquashEase` (how deep, how long and in what shape: sharp, pulse, soft or bouncy), `jumpGroundTime` and `jumpGroundEase` (how long the deepest squash is held on the ground, and how the weight settles through it), and `jumpRiseTime` / `jumpRiseEase` for the way back from the deepest squash to the body's own shape — a landing is the press, the hold and the rise, each with its own time and easing; squash and stretch scale about the body's base, so a landing keeps the feet on the ground. The breathing cycle integrates its phase, so its period can slow into sleep without the body pulsing through the switch. A click's jump lands with a squash half as long again as an idle jump's (`jumpClickSquashTime`, 0.24 s, a Studio slider) — with a Studio group for them. The head's idle wander moves as a lightly damped spring instead of an exponential approach, so each turn starts and ends softly.

A React Native port lives at `ports/react-native` (`bot-avatars-native`, local-only, plus an Expo example): the same rig imported from this package, drawn with react-native-skia and Reanimated — the slice stack, the face, the whirl, and the plastic material baked with `bakeBotAvatarForm` and lit per pixel in an SkSL port of `buildMatcap`.

A SwiftUI port for iOS 17+ lives at `ports/ios` (BotAvatarsKit, a SwiftPM package, and the BotAvatarsDemo app): the same rig, renderer and plastic material drawn into a `Canvas`, with a tap for the click and a finger drag for the pointer, plus `BotAvatarStage`, one canvas for a whole roster.
